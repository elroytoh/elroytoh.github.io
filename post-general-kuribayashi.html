General Kuribayashi

3 July 2026

I’ve been thinking about General Kuribayashi lately—especially the letters he wrote while commanding Iwo Jima. Watching Letters from Iwo Jima gave me a perspective I didn’t expect.

People often say history is written by the victors, but these letters feel like the opposite. They aren’t propaganda or grand speeches. They’re intimate, human messages—words from a father, a brother, and a husband writing to the family he knew he would never see again. Even as defeat closed in, Kuribayashi was determined to hold the island as long as possible, not out of triumph, but to delay the suffering that would eventually reach Tokyo—where his own loved ones lived.

As a Chinese person, I carry the painful weight of Japan’s occupation as a lasting, haunting part of history. And yet, reading these letters, I can’t help but feel empathy for someone who gave his life to a cause he may not have believed in, driven by one stubborn, familiar hope: to spare his family from harm.

Moving and getting rid of stuff, trying to declutter and also finding out what works for me.
